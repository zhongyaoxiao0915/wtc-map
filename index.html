Mapping the Unbuilt: the World Trade Center site as a record of negotiation

The sixteen acres as one answer among many. Solid lines mark what stands; dashed, translucent shapes mark what was proposed between 2002 and 2005 and not built. Switch layers on and off to assemble another Lower Manhattan.

Every shape is a schematic redrawing after the teams’ own 2002 presentations: approximate, stylized and not to scale. This map is of designs and decisions, not of loss.

Decisions, 2001–2016

Negotiations with no footprint

July 2001: Silverstein’s 99-year lease, signed weeks before the attack 2004: insurance trials over whether the attack was one event or two 2006: lease renegotiated; Port Authority takes over the Freedom Tower Chinatown and Battery Park City residents, recovery workers, small businesses: little purchase on the process

Limitations

  • Designs and decisions, not loss. This map is not dedicated to the deceased and the implicated victims. I envision it as a map of designs and decisions, not of loss (though it matters profoundly), and it says so plainly rather than gesturing at the victims through symbols.
  • Negotiations with no spatial trace. It cannot show the negotiations that left no spatial trace, such as the insurance litigation over whether the attack counted as one event or two, or the terms of Silverstein’s lease. These shaped the site as much as any drawing did, but they have no footprint; the map can only mark them on the timeline strip and note their absence.
  • The question of voice. Chinatown and Battery Park City residents, recovery workers, and small businesses had little purchase on the process, and the competition entries barely register them. By mapping only designs, the map risks repeating the competition’s own preference for form over people.
